Ontology Specification Draft
The Historic Structure Ontology (HSTO) provides a formal knowledge representation of the physical configuration of historic masonry aggregates, explicitly modelling the relationships among their structural components. The ontology introduces hsto:StructuralPart as its central class, from which two main subclasses are derived: hsto:Facade and hsto:HorizontalStructure. Facades are further linked to historic openings and their heritage-specific components — jambs, sills, lintels, arches — extending standard BIM schemas with historically grounded construction details. Horizontal structures are decomposed into timber floors and masonry vaults, with timber floors further articulated into beams, joists, and planks. HSTO also models structural connections as explicit first-class objects, with two prevalent subtypes — quoins and ties — and a connection quality property to support FEM modelling strategies. Each structural part can be associated with datation information, accessibility assessments, damage instances (via DOT), and traditional construction techniques (via HSV), enabling a comprehensive and interoperable description of historic masonry buildings for structural analysis and heritage documentation.
The structural characterisation of historic masonry buildings requires the representation of building elements and construction details that are systematically absent from modern digital construction schemas such as IFC. Features such as quoins, relieving arches, historic openings with their datation, and the variety of horizontal structural assemblies found in historic fabrics — from timber floors to masonry vaults — demand a dedicated ontological framework that goes beyond what standard BIM vocabularies currently offer. The Historic Structure Ontology (HSTO) addresses this gap by providing a formal representation of the physical configuration of masonry aggregates and the relationships among their structural components. The ontology is organised around the class hsto:StructuralPart, which serves as the main category for modelling individual structural components. From this class, two primary subclasses are derived: hsto:Facade, which captures the exterior wall surface of a structural unit including its opening layout and masonry typology, and hsto:HorizontalStructure, which models floor and roof assemblies providing diaphragm rigidity to the aggregate. HSTO introduces structural connections as explicit first-class objects rather than implicit geometric contacts, allowing the qualitative assessment of connection effectiveness to directly inform structural modelling choices. The ontology further enriches standard BIM entities from the Building Element Ontology (BEO) with heritage-specific opening components — sill, jamb, lintel, arch — and links structural parts to damage descriptions via the Damage Typology Ontology (DOT) and to traditional construction techniques via the Historic Survey Ontology (HSV). HSTO is published at https://w3id.org/hsto and is available in Turtle serialisation under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence.
